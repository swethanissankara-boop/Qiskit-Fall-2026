## 🤔 Quick Q&A

**❓ What is a quantum kernel?**
A way to measure how *similar* two data points are, using a quantum circuit. Each point is encoded into a quantum state, and the overlap between two states becomes the similarity score.

**❓ Why use a quantum computer for that?**
Quantum states live in a huge space that is hard to simulate classically. The hope is that data which looks tangled in normal space becomes easier to separate there.

**❓ So is it faster than a normal computer?**
Not proven yet. Today it is a research tool, not a replacement for classical ML. That is exactly why we test it honestly.

**❓ Does the quantum computer do all the work?**
No. It is a hybrid: the quantum circuit builds the similarity table, and a classical SVM does the classifying.


**❓ What is "heavy-hex"?**
A chip layout used by IBM, where qubits form hexagon-like patterns with limited connections. It reduces crosstalk and errors while keeping routing manageable.

**❓ What is noise, and why should I care?**
Every gate and measurement has a small error. Longer circuits collect more errors, so fewer gates means better answers.
